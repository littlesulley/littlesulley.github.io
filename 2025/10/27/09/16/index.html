Camera Screen Space Constraints

Sulley
2025-10-27
游戏 - 相机
UE, 数学, 相机, 计算机, 随笔

Most gameplay cameras follow a single world-space pivot, maintaining a fixed offset from it. Sometimes, though, we want the pivot of interest (POI) to appear at specific screen positions. For example, in some lock-on systems, the character’s POI is placed on the left side of the screen while the enemy’s POI is placed on the right. This post explains how to translate or rotate the camera so the POI lands at the desired screen-space position.

Problem Formulation

Formally, we’d like to translate or rotate the camera so that the world space POI p=(px,py,pz)\mathbf{p}=(p_x,p_y,p_z)p=(px​,py​,pz​) lands at the desired normalized screen-space position (sx,sy)(s_x,s_y)(sx​,sy​) where sx,sy∈[−0.5,0.5]s_x,s_y\in[-0.5, 0.5]sx​,sy​∈[−0.5,0.5]. Coordiante (0,0)(0,0)(0,0) represents screen center and (0.5,0.5)(0.5,0.5)(0.5,0.5) is the top-right corner.

We can achieve this by either translating or rotating the camera, as introduced below.

Camera Translation

Displacing the POI by camera translation is straightforward: keep the camera’s rotation fixed, choose a desired forward distance DDD from the camera to the pivot along the local X axis, then slide the camera along its local Y and Z axes until the POI reaches the target screen position. We follow Unreal’s coordinate conventions, where the forward unit vector is the X axis, right Y axis and up Z axis. The desired forward distance must be specified because the view volume is a perspective frustum, DDD determines how screen offsets map to camera‑space offsets.

The following figure shows the relationship of the desired screen space position sxs_xsx​, horizontal field of view and the forward distance DDD, in camera’s local space projected onto the XY plane:

In one word, the POI’s Y axis coordinate must be 2sxDtan⁡(FOV/2)2s_xD\tan(\text{FOV}/2)2sx​Dtan(FOV/2) in the camera’s local space. Given this, we can translate the camera in its local Y axis so that this constraint can be met.

The vertical case is analogous: with normalized screen Y axis position sys_ysy​ and vertical field of view vFOV\text{vFOV}vFOV, the required camera space Z coordinate is 2syDtan⁡(vFOV/2)2s_yD\tan(\text{vFOV}/2)2sy​Dtan(vFOV/2), where tan⁡(vFOV/2)=tan⁡(FOV/2)/AspectRatio\tan(\text{vFOV}/2) = \tan(\text{FOV}/2)/\text{AspectRatio}tan(vFOV/2)=tan(FOV/2)/AspectRatio.

Camera Rotation

Camera rotation is more complex. We start by assuming the desired rotation’s forward direction is (cos⁡θcos⁡ϕ,cos⁡θsin⁡ϕ,sin⁡θ)⊤(\cos\theta\cos\phi,\cos\theta\sin\phi,\sin\theta)^\top(cosθcosϕ,cosθsinϕ,sinθ)⊤ where θ∈[−π/2,π/2]\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]θ∈[−π/2,π/2] is the pitch and ϕ∈[−π,π]\phi\in[-\pi,\pi]ϕ∈[−π,π] is the yaw. Then, the inverse rotation matrix can be represented as:

R−1=[cos⁡θcos⁡ϕcos⁡θsin⁡ϕsin⁡θ−sin⁡ϕcos⁡ϕ0−sin⁡θcos⁡ϕ−sin⁡θsin⁡ϕcos⁡θ]\mathbf{R}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta\cos\phi & \cos\theta\sin\phi & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\phi & \cos\phi & 0 \\ -\sin\theta\cos\phi & -\sin\theta\sin\phi & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} R−1=⎣⎢⎡​cosθcosϕ−sinϕ−sinθcosϕ​cosθsinϕcosϕ−sinθsinϕ​sinθ0cosθ​⎦⎥⎤​

The inverse rotation matrix R−1\mathbf{R}^{-1}R−1 maps a world-space vector into the rotation’s local space. Hence, it maps the directional vector from camera position to POI, i.e., d=p−c\mathbf{d}=\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{c}d=p−c, to the local direction d∗=R−1d\mathbf{d}^*=\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{d}d∗=R−1d.

According to what we derived in last section, d∗\mathbf{d}^*d∗ must satisfy:

2sxtan⁡(FOV/2)(dxcos⁡θcos⁡ϕ+dycos⁡θsin⁡ϕ+dzsin⁡θ)=−dxsin⁡ϕ+dycos⁡ϕ2sytan⁡(vFOV/2)(dxcos⁡θcos⁡ϕ+dycos⁡θsin⁡ϕ+dzsin⁡θ)=−dxsin⁡θcos⁡ϕ−dysin⁡θsin⁡ϕ+dzcos⁡θ\begin{aligned} 2s_x\tan(\text{FOV}/2)(d_x\cos\theta\cos\phi + d_y\cos\theta\sin\phi + d_z\sin\theta) &= -d_x\sin\phi + d_y\cos\phi\\ 2s_y\tan(\text{vFOV}/2)(d_x\cos\theta\cos\phi + d_y\cos\theta\sin\phi + d_z\sin\theta) &= -d_x\sin\theta\cos\phi -d_y\sin\theta\sin\phi + d_z\cos\theta \end{aligned} 2sx​tan(FOV/2)(dx​cosθcosϕ+dy​cosθsinϕ+dz​sinθ)2sy​tan(vFOV/2)(dx​cosθcosϕ+dy​cosθsinϕ+dz​sinθ)​=−dx​sinϕ+dy​cosϕ=−dx​sinθcosϕ−dy​sinθsinϕ+dz​cosθ​

Summary

It took me quite a while to work through this problem. I first tried to derive an analytic (closed‑form) rotation, but the results consistently deviated from the ground truth. Suspecting a closed form might not be attainable, I switched to an iterative solver, which produced exact results.
