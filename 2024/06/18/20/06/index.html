The Spring Mass Damper System and Its Application On Gameplay Camera

Sulley
2024-06-18
数学 - 分析
Lerp, Spring, 微分方程, 插值, 数学, 相机, 线性代数, 计算机, 随笔

In this last post I introduced frame-independent interpolation and reached the conclusion of using the exponential function to interpolate values. It has a wide variety of uses in video games, e.g., be used to damp animation motions which constitute the building blocks of what you see when playing games. In today’s post, I will introduce a related and more popular technique to damp values, that is, the spring mass damper system.

Deriving the Spring Mass Damper System

The very famous Hooke’s law states that the force F\mathbf{F}F needed to extend or compress a spring by some distance x\mathbf{x}x scales linearly with respect to that distance by a factor kkk. That is,

F=−kx\mathbf{F}=-k\mathbf{x} F=−kx

From the other side, Newton’s Second Law tells us that the force that causes one object to move is the product of its mass and its acceleration:

F=ma\mathbf{F}=m\mathbf{a} F=ma

Connecting these two equations:

−kxt−dvt=matmat+dvt+kxt=0at+2ζωvt+ω2xt=0 (ω2=k/m, ζ=d/2mω)\begin{aligned} -k\mathbf{x}_t{\color{red}-d\mathbf{v}_t} &= m\mathbf{a}_t\\ m\mathbf{a}_t+d\mathbf{v}_t+k\mathbf{x}_t &= 0\\ \mathbf{a}_t+2\zeta\omega\mathbf{v}_t+\omega^2\mathbf{x}_t&=0 ~(\omega^2=k/m, ~\zeta=d/2m\omega) \end{aligned} −kxt​−dvt​mat​+dvt​+kxt​at​+2ζωvt​+ω2xt​​=mat​=0=0 (ω2=k/m, ζ=d/2mω)​

An additional term of −dvt-d\mathbf{v}_t−dvt​ is added serving as the damper. The damper’s force is proportional to the velocity.

ω\omegaω is called the undamped natural frequency (named undamped as it comes only from kkk) and ζ\zetaζ is the damping ratio. We will explain these two terms soon later.

Solving this differential equation we have:

xt=C1e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1)+C2e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1)\mathbf{x}_t=C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}+C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)} xt​=C1​e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1​)+C2​e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1​)

This equation is quite intuitive:

  • When ζ=0\zeta=0ζ=0 (Undamped), the solution becomes xt=C1sin⁡(ωt)+C2cos⁡(ωt)\mathbf{x}_t=C_1\sin(\omega t)+C_2\cos(\omega t)xt​=C1​sin(ωt)+C2​cos(ωt), a pure harmonic oscillator.
  • When 0<ζ<10<\zeta<10<ζ<1 (Underdamped), the solution becomes a sum of two decaying oscillations, both decayed by e−ωζt\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}e−ωζt. Therefore, when a spring is underdamped, it will oscillate and eventually converge to its initial state. When this value is smaller (closer to zero), the spring will oscillate for a longer time.
  • When ζ=1\zeta=1ζ=1 (Critically damped), the solution is simply an exponential function xt=(C1+C2)e−ωt\mathbf{x}_t=(C_1+C_2)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}xt​=(C1​+C2​)e−ωt. We get to the same result as in our last article.
  • When ζ>1\zeta>1ζ>1 (Overdamped), the solution is simply a sum of two decaying exponentials with no oscillation. In this case, the spring will return slowly to its relaxing position and generally the larger ζ\zetaζ is, the slower it will be.

Assuming the initial values x0=xˉ\mathbf{x}_0=\bar{\mathbf{x}}x0​=xˉ and v0=vˉ\mathbf{v}_0=\bar{\mathbf{v}}v0​=vˉ, we can determine C1C_1C1​ and C2C_2C2​ for ζ>1\zeta>1ζ>1:

{C1+C2=xˉ(ζ+ζ2−1)C1+(ζ−ζ2−1)C2=−vˉ/ω⇒{C1=−vˉ/ω−(ζ−ζ2−1)xˉ2ζ2−1C2=xˉ−C1\begin{cases} C_1+C_2&=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\\ (\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})C_1 + (\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})C_2 &=-\bar{\mathbf{v}}/\omega \end{cases} \Rightarrow \begin{cases} C_1 = \dfrac{-\bar{\mathbf{v}}/\omega-(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{2\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}}\\ C_2 = \bar{\mathbf{x}} - C_1 \end{cases} {C1​+C2​(ζ+ζ2−1​)C1​+(ζ−ζ2−1​)C2​​=xˉ=−vˉ/ω​⇒⎩⎪⎪⎨⎪⎪⎧​C1​=2ζ2−1​−vˉ/ω−(ζ−ζ2−1​)xˉ​C2​=xˉ−C1​​

For ζ=1\zeta=1ζ=1 we immediately have:

xt=xˉe−ωt\mathbf{x}_t=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t} xt​=xˉe−ωt

For 0<ζ<10<\zeta<10<ζ<1, we can first compute xt\mathbf{x}_txt​’s two real solutions and take their linear combination. The two real solutions are e−ωζtcos⁡(ω1−ζ2t)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\cos(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)e−ωζtcos(ω1−ζ2​t) and e−ωζtsin⁡(ω1−ζ2t)\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\sin(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)e−ωζtsin(ω1−ζ2​t). Taking their linear combination and solving the coefficients:

xt=C1e−ωζtcos⁡(ω1−ζ2t)+C2e−ωζtsin⁡(ω1−ζ2t)x0=C1=xˉv0=−ωζC1+ω1−ζ2C2=vˉC1=xˉC2=vˉ+ωζxˉω1−ζ2\begin{aligned} \mathbf{x}_t &= C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\cos(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t) + C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\sin(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)\\ \mathbf{x}_0 &= C_1=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\\ \mathbf{v}_0 &= -\omega\zeta C_1+\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}C_2=\bar{\mathbf{v}}\\ C_1 &= \bar{\mathbf{x}}\\ C_2 &= \frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\omega\zeta\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} \end{aligned} xt​x0​v0​C1​C2​​=C1​e−ωζtcos(ω1−ζ2​t)+C2​e−ωζtsin(ω1−ζ2​t)=C1​=xˉ=−ωζC1​+ω1−ζ2​C2​=vˉ=xˉ=ω1−ζ2​vˉ+ωζxˉ​​

So the final equation is:

xt=xˉe−ωζtcos⁡(ω1−ζ2t)+vˉ+ωζxˉω1−ζ2e−ωζtsin⁡(ω1−ζ2t)\mathbf{x}_t=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\cos(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)+\frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\omega\zeta\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\sin(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t) xt​=xˉe−ωζtcos(ω1−ζ2​t)+ω1−ζ2​vˉ+ωζxˉ​e−ωζtsin(ω1−ζ2​t)

For ζ=0\zeta=0ζ=0, we substitute the initial values x0=xˉ\mathbf{x}_0=\bar{\mathbf{x}}x0​=xˉ and v0=vˉ\mathbf{v}_0=\bar{\mathbf{v}}v0​=vˉ into the equation and get C1=vˉ/ωC_1=\bar{\mathbf{v}}/\omegaC1​=vˉ/ω and C2=xˉC_2=\bar{\mathbf{x}}C2​=xˉ. The expression then becomes:

xt=vˉωsin⁡(ωt)+xˉcos⁡(ωt)\mathbf{x}_t=\frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)+\bar{\mathbf{x}}\cos(\omega t) xt​=ωvˉ​sin(ωt)+xˉcos(ωt)

Work done!

Okay, let’s summarize what we have reached so far. According to the value ζ\zetaζ, a spring-mass-damper system can be categorized into four classes:

  • Undamped where ζ=0\zeta=0ζ=0. In this case, the trajectory of the spring is simply a harmonic oscillation. Given initial values x0=xˉ\mathbf{x}_0=\bar{\mathbf{x}}x0​=xˉ and v0=vˉ\mathbf{v}_0=\bar{\mathbf{v}}v0​=vˉ, the trajectory and velocity equations can be expressed as:

xt=vˉωsin⁡(ωt)+xˉcos⁡(ωt)vt=vˉcos⁡(ωt)−ωxˉsin⁡(ωt)\begin{aligned} \mathbf{x}_t&=\frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}}{\omega}\sin(\omega t)+\bar{\mathbf{x}}\cos(\omega t)\\ \mathbf{v}_t&=\bar{\mathbf{v}}\cos(\omega t)-\omega\bar{\mathbf{x}}\sin(\omega t) \end{aligned} xt​vt​​=ωvˉ​sin(ωt)+xˉcos(ωt)=vˉcos(ωt)−ωxˉsin(ωt)​

  • Underdamped where 0<ζ<10<\zeta<10<ζ<1. In this case, the spring performs a decaying oscillation. Its trajectory and velocity equations are kind of complex:

xt=xˉe−ωζtcos⁡(ω1−ζ2t)+vˉ+ωζxˉω1−ζ2e−ωζtsin⁡(ω1−ζ2t)vt=−ωζxt+(vˉ+ωζxˉ)e−ωζtcos⁡(ω1−ζ2t)−xˉω1−ζ2e−ωζtsin⁡(ω1−ζ2t)\begin{aligned} \mathbf{x}_t&=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\cos(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)+\frac{\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\omega\zeta\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\sin(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)\\ \mathbf{v}_t&=-\omega\zeta\mathbf{x}_t+(\bar{\mathbf{v}}+\omega\zeta\bar{\mathbf{x}})\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\cos(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t)-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega\zeta t}\sin(\omega\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}t) \end{aligned} xt​vt​​=xˉe−ωζtcos(ω1−ζ2​t)+ω1−ζ2​vˉ+ωζxˉ​e−ωζtsin(ω1−ζ2​t)=−ωζxt​+(vˉ+ωζxˉ)e−ωζtcos(ω1−ζ2​t)−xˉω1−ζ2​e−ωζtsin(ω1−ζ2​t)​

  • Critically damped where ζ=1\zeta=1ζ=1. In this case, the spring will get to its relaxing state as soon as possible without oscillation. The trajectory and velocity equations are simply exponential functions:

xt=xˉe−ωtvt=−ωxt\begin{aligned} \mathbf{x}_t&=\bar{\mathbf{x}}\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t}\\ \mathbf{v}_t&=-\omega\mathbf{x}_t \end{aligned} xt​vt​​=xˉe−ωt=−ωxt​​

  • Overdamped where ζ>1\zeta>1ζ>1. The spring will return slowly to its relaxing position. The trajectory and velocity equations are:

xt=C1e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1)+C2e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1)vt=−ω(ζ+ζ2−1)C1e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1)−ω(ζ−ζ2−1)C2e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1)C1=−vˉ/ω−(ζ−ζ2−1)xˉ2ζ2−1C2=xˉ−C1\begin{aligned} \mathbf{x}_t&=C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}+C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}\\ \mathbf{v}_t&=-\omega\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}-\omega\left(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}\\ C_1&=\dfrac{-\bar{\mathbf{v}}/\omega-(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})\bar{\mathbf{x}}}{2\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}}\\ C_2&=\bar{\mathbf{x}}-C_1 \end{aligned} xt​vt​C1​C2​​=C1​e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1​)+C2​e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1​)=−ω(ζ+ζ2−1​)C1​e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1​)−ω(ζ−ζ2−1​)C2​e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1​)=2ζ2−1​−vˉ/ω−(ζ−ζ2−1​)xˉ​=xˉ−C1​​

Application On Gameplay Camera

In this section, let’s discuss one application of the spring-mass-damper system on video games, the gameplay camera, particularly the third person camera.

A third person camera tracks the player at a fixed distance. You can imagine the player is the origin and it’s dragging the camera back and forth, just like a spring right? The relaxing distance is the length the camera tries to keep away from the player. When the player goes outwards from the camera, the spring extends; when the player goes inwards to the camera, the spring compresses. Simple to imagine.

Implementation

The implementation is quite simple: just carrying the formulas here and substituting the numbers into. That’s all of it!

Input is the distance to pass through, or the initial position xˉ\bar{\mathbf{x}}xˉ. Output is the distance to pass for this frame. Frequency is ω\omegaω and DampRatio is ζ\zetaζ. CurrentVelocity is the initial velocity vˉ\bar{\mathbf{v}}vˉ and OutVelocity is the velocity at timestep DeltaSeconds, i.e., the velocity when this frame completes.

One thing to note here is that the OutVelocity computed at this frame will be used as the CurrentVelocity for the next frame in order to synchronize initial values at consecutive time steps.

Spring Mass Damper System is Framerate-Independent

The most interesting fact about the spring-mass-damper system is: it’s framerate-independent. Actually, we can prove the following statement:

For any given t1,t2>0t_1,t_2>0t1​,t2​>0, the result produced by sequentially applying the spring-mass-damper system on t1,t2t_1, t_2t1​,t2​, is equal to the result produced by applying once on t1+t2t_1+t_2t1​+t2​, assuming with the same initial values xˉ\bar{\mathbf{x}}xˉ and vˉ\bar{\mathbf{v}}vˉ.

That is, if we rewrite the spring-mass-damper function as:

f(t,xˉ,vˉ)=C1e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1)+C2e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1)f(t, \bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})=C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)}+C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t\left(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1}\right)} f(t,xˉ,vˉ)=C1​e−ωt(ζ+ζ2−1​)+C2​e−ωt(ζ−ζ2−1​)

where fff is a function depending on ttt and the two initial values, we claim that:

f(t1+t2,xˉ,vˉ)=f(t2,f(t1,xˉ,vˉ),f′(t1,xˉ,vˉ))\color{red}{ f(t_1+t_2,\bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})=f(t_2, f(t_1, \bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}}), f'(t_1, \bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})) } f(t1​+t2​,xˉ,vˉ)=f(t2​,f(t1​,xˉ,vˉ),f′(t1​,xˉ,vˉ))

Hence, fff is framerate-independent.

To prove this equality, we can first expand the left side term:

LHS=f(t1+t2,xˉ,vˉ)=C1e−ωt1(ζ+ζ2−1)⋅e−ωt2(ζ+ζ2−1)+C2e−ωt1(ζ−ζ2−1)⋅e−ωt2(ζ−ζ2−1)\begin{aligned} \text{LHS} &= f(t_1+t_2,\bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})\\ &= C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_1(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})}\cdot \mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_2(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})}\\ &+ C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_1(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})}\cdot \mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_2(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})} \end{aligned} LHS​=f(t1​+t2​,xˉ,vˉ)=C1​e−ωt1​(ζ+ζ2−1​)⋅e−ωt2​(ζ+ζ2−1​)+C2​e−ωt1​(ζ−ζ2−1​)⋅e−ωt2​(ζ−ζ2−1​)​

Then we expand the right hand side:

RHS=Cˉ1e−ωt2(ζ+ζ2−1)+Cˉ2e−ωt2(ζ−ζ2−1)\text{RHS}=\bar{C}_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_2(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})}+\bar{C}_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_2(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})} RHS=Cˉ1​e−ωt2​(ζ+ζ2−1​)+Cˉ2​e−ωt2​(ζ−ζ2−1​)

Note that Cˉ1,Cˉ2\bar{C}_1, \bar{C}_2Cˉ1​,Cˉ2​ are calculate based on the new initial values, and are different from C1,C2C_1, C_2C1​,C2​.

Comparing two sides, they are equal when:

Cˉ1=C1e−ωt1(ζ+ζ2−1)Cˉ2=C2e−ωt1(ζ−ζ2−1)\begin{aligned} \bar{C}_1&=C_1\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_1(\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})}\\ \bar{C}_2&=C_2\mathrm{e}^{-\omega t_1(\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})} \end{aligned} Cˉ1​Cˉ2​​=C1​e−ωt1​(ζ+ζ2−1​)=C2​e−ωt1​(ζ−ζ2−1​)​

This holds because for the new Cˉ1,Cˉ2\bar{C}_1, \bar{C}_2Cˉ1​,Cˉ2​, they satisfy the following equations:

Cˉ1+Cˉ2=f(t,xˉ,vˉ)(ζ+ζ2−1)Cˉ1+(ζ−ζ2−1)Cˉ2=−f′(t,xˉ,vˉ)/ω\begin{aligned} \bar{C}_1+\bar{C}_2&=f(t, \bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})\\ (\zeta+\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})\bar{C}_1 + (\zeta-\sqrt{\zeta^2-1})\bar{C}_2 &=-f'(t, \bar{\mathbf{x}}, \bar{\mathbf{v}})/\omega \end{aligned} Cˉ1​+Cˉ2​(ζ+ζ2−1​)Cˉ1​+(ζ−ζ2−1​)Cˉ2​​=f(t,xˉ,vˉ)=−f′(t,xˉ,vˉ)/ω​

This is obviously correct for our Cˉ1,Cˉ2\bar{C}_1, \bar{C}_2Cˉ1​,Cˉ2​. Proof completes.

You may ask you’re only considering the overdamped case, what about other cases. Well, you can verify that for all other cases, the claim still holds true. The proofs are left as exercises for you.

Conclusion

The spring-mass-damper system is intriguing. It’s simple, effective and easy-to-implement. It has a very wide application on control systems, animations and architectures. The topic discussed today is only a small fraction of the spring system. I will cover more of it in future days. Hope you enjoy reading this blog post.
